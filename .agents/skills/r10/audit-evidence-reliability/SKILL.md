# Audit evidence reliability

## Use when

Use this Skill while constructing an audit scenario in which the worker must decide whether information can support an audit conclusion, especially when management-produced reports, system extracts, or conflicting records are involved.

## Objective

Create evidence that lets a candidate explain what is reliable, what needs corroboration, and what remains unsupported. The resulting work product should distinguish evidence quality from mere agreement between files.

## Professional judgment to preserve

- Consider the source, nature, and circumstances of each item of evidence, including whether company-produced information is accurate and complete for its intended use.
- Make corroboration, system lineage, or control evidence consequential when the scenario depends on internally generated information.
- Preserve the possibility that available evidence is insufficient; a defensible follow-up may be preferable to an unsupported conclusion.
- Keep normal operating evidence alongside the conflict so the task requires assessment rather than locating an explicitly marked exception.

## Serious failure modes

- Treating a management summary or internally generated report as self-validating.
- Equating a numerical match with reliable, complete audit evidence.
- Revealing the answer through conclusion labels or teacher-only treatment in candidate material.
- Turning the work into a generic arithmetic or formatting exercise with no evidence-reliability judgment.
