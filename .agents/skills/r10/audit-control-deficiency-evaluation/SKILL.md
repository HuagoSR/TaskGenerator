# Audit control deficiency evaluation

## Purpose

Use this Skill to shape an occupationally credible integrated-audit situation that requires severity judgment. The work belongs primarily to an audit senior, with an audit manager and engagement partner as reviewers and escalation recipients. Other participants may supply evidence or context, but they should not pre-decide the auditor's conclusion.

## Situation frame

Trigger the work when walkthrough or control-testing observations are mature enough for a deficiency-evaluation discussion. Give the professional a reason to assess observations both separately and together, especially where they affect related accounts, disclosures, assertions, processes, or compensating controls.

Use natural source-system and engagement materials such as walkthrough narratives, inquiry notes, control test records, relevant risk and process context, system reports, remediation descriptions, and prior observations where genuinely relevant. Preserve enough identifiers, dates, scope, and provenance for a reviewer to connect an observation to its evidence, without evaluative labels that disclose the result.

## Judgment to elicit

Require a reviewable rationale about reasonable possibility, potential magnitude, and the interaction of related deficiencies. The absence or presence of a detected misstatement must not settle severity by itself. Distinguish observed facts, management representations, corroborating evidence, unresolved points, and professional reasoning.

Plausible gaps include incomplete population support, unclear frequency or duration, uncertain affected assertions, missing compensating-control evidence, remediation that is recent or untested, inconsistent control ownership, and observations whose combined effect has not been addressed. Leave the candidate to decide aggregation, severity, evidence weight, escalation, and necessary follow-up.

## Construction boundaries

Do not encode severity in filenames, headings, answer labels, comments, or management language. Do not prescribe a candidate procedure sequence, a teacher solution path, or a fixed deliverable layout. Keep the evidence rich enough for synthesis rather than reducing the work to a checklist or one-row exception lookup.
